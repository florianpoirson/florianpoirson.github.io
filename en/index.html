The approach

A language is not a neutral code you transpose. It is a set of conventions, register, humour, silence, how you say no, that decide how you are heard. Flawless grammar is no substitute for them.

Start from your situation

Why this language, who you are addressing, what you need to obtain, and by when.

Work on the culture

The material comes from the target culture. That is what turns correct English into the right English.

Close the last gap

Rhythm, intonation, allusion and what goes unsaid: what separates a very good level from a native one.

Executive level

For executives, experts and diplomats: the language is no longer a skill to acquire but an instrument of work, and the smallest approximation costs credibility.

  • Executive Speaking & Leadership

    Public speaking, chairing meetings, handling objections at board level, the art of the pitch. We work on how the argument is built as much as on the voice that carries it.

  • Negotiation and diplomacy

    Mastering understatement, decoding native irony, techniques of influence, and the art of firm disagreement that leaves the relationship intact.

  • Strategic writing

    Financial reports, contracts, unattributed policy notes, press releases, sensitive correspondence: written English without a single false note, in the precise register the reader expects.

  • Accent reduction and delivery

    Intonation, rhythm, pace, stress, pauses, and the cultural ease that removes the barrier before the first substantive word is spoken.

Background

Séverine Williams holds a PhD from Harvard University and the French agrégation in English. She has taught for twenty years at Harvard, Boston College, Bryn Mawr College, the Institut français in London, the Sorbonne, the Lycée Louis-le-Grand and the French international school in Marrakech. Her way of teaching languages comes from that movement between five countries.
